## Notes

Plaza de Armas is a Queretaro-based newspaper and digital outlet branded as "El Periodico de Queretaro." Its own legal and privacy pages identify Medios Aqropolis S.A. de C.V. as the responsible/rights-holding entity for the site and its publications.

The outlet's own anniversary/history articles give a two-step founding chronology. A 2020 anniversary column says Plaza de Armas began as a web portal in January 2010 and became "El Periodico de Queretaro" on July 16, 2010. A 2026 anniversary column describes it as the first outlet born online and then moved to paper. A July 2026 article on the inauguration of new offices says the newspaper evolved from its 2010 founding and refers to Sergio Arturo Venegas Ramirez as founder; the piece also names Sergio Arturo Venegas Alarcon as director general.

The project is explicitly described as family-linked in the 2020 history article: it mentions Magdalena Ramirez, Alejandra Vilchis, Paulina Venegas, Rene Venegas, Fernando Venegas, Sergio Arturo Venegas Alarcon, Lucy and Armando Venegas Ramirez, Fernanda Venegas Vilchis and Mario Carranza Venegas as part of the founding/early project.

Sergio Arturo Venegas Ramirez, known as "El Armero," was a central editorial figure. Later Plaza de Armas pieces and a 2026 state journalism prize announcement identify him as cofounder/director editorial and a major Queretaro journalist. He died in 2025.

## Ideology / Alignment Evidence

Recommended coding: `local/regional political watchdog; plural/open by self-description; current national alignment unclear`.

Do not code as simply `aligned` or `opp` from current evidence. Plaza de Armas' own anniversary articles stress openness to all parties and plural opinion pages. The 2020 anniversary piece says its pages were open to all political signs; a 2026 column says it follows PAN, Morena, PVEM, PRI and other actors, and claims similar treatment of interview subjects. That supports a `plural/local-watchdog` self-description.

There is evidence of conflict with local state governments, but this is period-specific rather than an ideology label. The 2020 history article says the outlet faced a boycott from the then-current governor's office; a 2018 article about Sergio Arturo Venegas Alarcon describes alleged persecution/boicot by the Queretaro state government for criticism. That suggests an anti-incumbent / watchdog posture in some periods, not a stable national partisan alignment.

The outlet has also covered and commented heavily on Queretaro's PAN-Morena contest. Some recent pieces note the strength of the PAN in Queretaro and discuss Morena challengers, but I did not find enough external evidence to code the outlet itself as PAN-aligned, Morena-aligned, pro-AMLO, or anti-AMLO.
