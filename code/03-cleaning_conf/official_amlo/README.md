# AMLO official transcript archive

This scraper downloads public government HTML directly. It uses no language
model and consumes no API credits. Existing AMLO CSVs, journalist/outlet
harmonization and analysis outputs are preserved.

```sh
.venv/bin/python code/03-cleaning_conf/official_amlo/scrape.py --workers 6
.venv/bin/python code/03-cleaning_conf/official_amlo/audit.py
```

The default destination is
`MEDIA_ROOT/data/02-conferences/official_source/full/`. Both the `articulos`
and `prensa` archive sections are scanned for conference pages published from
December 3, 2018 through September 30, 2024. This also discovers dates missing
from the local NOSTRODATA CSVs. Presidential/morning-conference titles are
selected; separate COVID and departmental briefings are recorded in
`excluded_archive_entries.json`. Local dates missing from the index are also
checked using the established official presidential-transcript URL pattern;
these require an exact title-date match. Archive index pages and transcript HTML are
cached with retrieval timestamps, source URLs and SHA-256 hashes. Rerunning
resumes from verified cached files. `--discover-only` collects indexes;
`--skip-discovery` reuses a completed discovery manifest.
`--rebuild-only` reparses saved HTML offline and regenerates exports without
making network requests.

The parser preserves every visible text node in the article body, including
text outside paragraph tags and fragments split by inline formatting. It
keeps `raw_text` alongside the text following an explicit uppercase speaker
label. Unlabeled openings remain unassigned and are flagged. Page dates are
taken from titles where available; publication-date fallbacks and conflicts
are flagged for review. Government transcripts may themselves contain errors.

Outputs:

- `raw/`: original HTML and provenance sidecars, including pages that need
  review or prove not to be transcripts.
- `indexes/` and `discovery.json`: source discovery records and completion state.
- `auxiliar/all_page_rows.parquet`: every successfully parsed page version.
- `auxiliar/transcript_rows.parquet`: one selected page per date; the longest
  parsed version is used when several exist. Duplicate dates are flagged.
- `auxiliar/source_comparison.csv`: original CSV versus official paragraph
  and word counts, with ordered exact-paragraph matches and source links.
- `auxiliar/original_csv_inventory.csv` and `original_date_conflicts.csv`:
  local file hashes and folder/filename date inconsistencies. Original dates
  are retained in comparisons; conflicts require review before joining data.
- `auxiliar/missing_official_dates.csv`, `additional_official_dates.csv` and
  `page_review.csv`: coverage and document-review queues.
- `auxiliar/preparation_audit.json`, `manifest.json` and `review/index.html`:
  progress/completion, provenance, counts, limitations and readable comparison.
- `auxiliar/integrity_and_comparison_audit.json` and
  `ranked_source_differences.csv`: independent saved-file checks and differences
  ranked by absolute word-count change; generated by `audit.py`.
- `auxiliar/aligned_speaker_label_differences.csv`: exact-text paragraph
  matches with different speaker labels. Many are harmless label conventions,
  not proven attribution errors.
- `auxiliar/official_repeated_text_review.csv`: possible repeated transcript
  blocks, detected by comparing the two halves of each document. This is a
  review heuristic, not automatic deduplication.

## Collection checked October 14, 2026

The saved archive contains 1,369 parsed pages covering 1,366 distinct dates.
All indexed candidate pages were downloaded. Against 1,390 distinct dates in
1,391 original CSV files, 90 dates remain unmatched and 66 official dates are
additional. Unsuccessful URL-pattern gap lookups remain in the manifest;
they do not imply failures to download indexed pages. Missing dates must not
be treated as days without a conference. The archive includes raw text that
still needs review before final analytical word counts or identity extraction.

Confirmed examples that motivate source review:

- December 6, 2023 and April 10, 2024: original CSVs have no transcript rows;
  official transcripts contain approximately 10,000 and 14,154 words.
- December 28, 2022 and December 9, 2020: original CSVs contain only 80 words,
  compared with 10,312 and 8,090 official words, respectively.
- January 31, 2020: the original CSV combines the morning conference with a
  separate Digital Strategy/Ministry of Economy briefing. The latter's
  official HTML is saved under `verification/raw/`; its opening matches the
  continuation after the morning transcript in the original CSV.
- March 10, 2022: the official page repeats a near-complete transcript block.
  Its raw text is preserved; do not count both blocks as actual speech.
- The February 19, 2019 folder contains a file labeled February 18, creating
  a duplicate original date. The original file is preserved and flagged.

Two official title dates are corrected using reviewed, hash-guarded rules:
January 17 to January 18, 2019, and December 17 to December 18, 2023. Both
publication dates and extensive exact paragraph matches support the corrected
dates. The original titles and correction notes remain in page metadata.

Paragraph differences can reflect spacing, splits/merges, headings, revisions
or omissions. They are not counts of missing questions. This scrape does not
rerun journalist/outlet extraction or transfer old numeric source-row IDs to
the new documents. Such a rerun should use the verified new source text.

There is a separate extraction limitation in `../extract_periodistas.py`:
that script discovers only `csv_por_participante/PREGUNTA.csv` and truncates
each paragraph to `MAX_CHARS_PER_ROW = 150`. Consequently, a close match in
full-transcript word counts does not establish complete journalist/outlet
coverage. This scrape does not alter or rerun that extraction script.
